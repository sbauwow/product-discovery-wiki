# Continuous Discovery

> Source: Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits* (2021). The operating cadence that makes [dual-track agile](dual-track-agile.md) actually work.

## The definition (Torres, verbatim spirit)

> At minimum weekly touchpoints with customers, by the team building the product, where they conduct small research activities, in pursuit of a desired outcome.

Five clauses, all load-bearing:

| Clause | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Weekly minimum** | Quarterly research forgets context between sessions; weekly forces the team to stay close to the customer |
| **Touchpoints with customers** | Not analytics, not stakeholder opinions — actual users |
| **By the team building** | Not outsourced to a research department; insight degrades through translation |
| **Small research activities** | 30-min interviews, 5-user usability tests — not 6-week studies |
| **Desired outcome** | Anchored to a measurable [outcome](outcomes-vs-outputs.md), not "understand users better" |

## The trio

The three roles that participate in every discovery activity:
1. **Product manager** — value/viability
2. **Designer** — usability
3. **Engineer (tech lead)** — feasibility

Maps directly to [Cagan's four risks](cagan-four-risks.md). Trio attends interviews together — different roles hear different things in the same conversation.

## Weekly rhythm (typical)

```
Mon  - 1-2 user interviews (45 min each), trio attends
Tue  - synthesis: update opportunity solution tree
Wed  - prototype review / experiment design
Thu  - 1-2 more interviews OR usability test on prototype
Fri  - team review: what changed in the tree this week?
```

## Recruiting at scale

The hardest operational problem. Solutions:
- **Recruit from product** — in-app intercept ("answer 3 questions, get $25") for active users
- **Continuous recruiting** — always have 2 weeks of interviews scheduled, not "let's recruit when we need to"
- **CRM partnership** — sales/CS pass warm contacts; team interviews them
- **Panel services** — User Interviews, Respondent.io for breadth

## Why "continuous" beats "occasional"

| Occasional research | Continuous discovery |
|---|---|
| Big quarterly study | Weekly small touchpoints |
| Specialist researcher | Whole trio |
| Insights as deliverable | Insights as input to OST |
| Decisions wait for results | Decisions update weekly |
| Forgetting between studies | Compounding context |

The compounding effect is the point. After 6 months of weekly interviews, the team has direct knowledge of ~150 users. That's a different organism than one that reads a research report.

## Anti-patterns

- **"We did interviews last quarter"** — context decayed; users changed; market changed
- **PM-only interviews** — engineer/designer don't hear the same thing the PM filters
- **Interviewing for validation** — asking "would you use X?" instead of probing past behavior. See [user interviews](user-interviews.md).
- **No tree update** — interviews happen but [opportunity tree](opportunity-solution-tree.md) doesn't change. Research ≠ discovery.
