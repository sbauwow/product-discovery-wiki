# Empowered Product Team

> Source: Marty Cagan, *Empowered* (2020). The staffing model that [dual-track agile](dual-track-agile.md) and [continuous discovery](continuous-discovery.md) require.

## Definition

> A team given **problems to solve** (outcomes), not **features to build** (outputs), with the autonomy and skill to figure out the best solution.

Empowered = (skilled trio) + (real autonomy) + (outcome-shaped goals) + (access to customers).

## The trio

| Role | Owns | Reduces risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Product Manager** | Value, viability | Will users buy? Does it work for the business? |
| **Product Designer** | Usability | Can users figure it out? |
| **Tech Lead / Eng** | Feasibility | Can we build it? |

Maps to [Cagan's four risks](cagan-four-risks.md). The trio is the smallest unit that can responsibly tackle all four.

## What "empowered" actually means

A team is empowered if **all** of these hold:

1. **Outcome goals**, not feature requests (see [outcomes vs outputs](outcomes-vs-outputs.md))
2. **Direct customer access** — they don't need permission to talk to users
3. **Discovery time** — at least 20% of capacity, ideally 30–40%
4. **Decision authority** within their problem space (no design-by-committee)
5. **Strategic context** — they understand company strategy, can push back on bad asks
6. **Skilled members** — strong PM, strong designer, strong tech lead

If even one of these is missing, the team is a [feature team](feature-vs-product-team.md), not empowered.

## Empowered vs feature team

| Empowered product team | Feature team |
|---|---|
| Given outcomes | Given features |
| Discovers solutions | Builds spec'd solutions |
| Strong PM | Project manager / BA |
| Engineers in discovery | Engineers as builders |
| Roadmap = bets | Roadmap = commitments |
| Failure to move metric is debug | Failure to ship on time is debug |

The distinction is the difference between a team that *thinks* and one that *implements*.

## What managers do for empowered teams

Cagan's reframe of management:
- **Coach** the trio (skill development)
- **Recruit** strong members (the skill is the precondition)
- **Set context** (strategy, not tactics)
- **Remove obstacles** (political cover)

NOT: assign features, micromanage solutions, run the team's discovery for them.

## Common failure modes

- **"Empowered" but with quarterly feature commitments** — autonomy on paper, mandate in practice. Most "agile transformation" companies live here.
- **No real PM** — project managers, BAs, or feature owners ≠ product managers
- **Design or engineering relegated to service role** — they should be co-equals, not consulted
- **No customer access** — sales/CS gatekeep, team operates on hearsay
- **Outcomes that aren't outcomes** — "improve onboarding" is not measurable

## Why it's hard

Empowered teams require:
- Executives willing to commit to outcomes, not output deadlines
- A leadership culture comfortable with experiments that fail
- Top-tier hiring (not always achievable)
- Reorgs that break long-standing political structures

Most companies say they want it; few accept the trade-offs.
