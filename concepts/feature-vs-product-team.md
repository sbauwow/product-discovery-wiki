# Feature Team vs Product Team

> Source: Marty Cagan. The most-cited org-design distinction in modern product.

## The distinction

| Dimension | Feature team | Product team |
|---|---|---|
| **What they're given** | A feature spec | A problem / outcome |
| **What they own** | Delivery | Outcome |
| **Discovery** | None or token | Continuous |
| **PM role** | Project manager / BA | Product manager |
| **Engineers** | Builders | Co-discoverers |
| **Designers** | Mockup makers | Co-discoverers |
| **Customer access** | Filtered through PM/sales | Direct |
| **Success metric** | On-time delivery | Outcome moved |
| **Failure looks like** | Late shipment | Metric didn't move |

## The mechanics

A feature team's input is a **roadmap of features**, decided elsewhere (by execs, sales, or a "product strategy team"). The team's job is execution. They have little authority to push back, redesign, or pivot.

A product team's input is an **outcome to move**. The team decides *how*. They run discovery, propose solutions, kill bad ideas, and own the result.

```
FEATURE TEAM:
  Strategy team → spec → team → ship → (no feedback loop)

PRODUCT TEAM:
  Strategy → outcome → team ⇄ discovery ⇄ users → ship → measure → adapt
```

## Why the distinction matters

Feature teams optimize delivery. Product teams optimize value. These look the same on a Gantt chart, diverge wildly in 12 months.

Feature team after a year:
- Shipped 30 features
- 3 moved metrics, 27 didn't
- Team can't tell which were which (no measurement loop)
- Backlog full of unvalidated requests

Product team after a year:
- Shipped 12 features (smaller volume)
- 8 moved metrics meaningfully
- Killed 30+ ideas in discovery before building
- Team has clear narrative of what worked and why

## Why companies have feature teams

It's not stupidity. Reasons:
- **Predictability** — execs want roadmap commitments
- **Coordination** — sales sells features, not outcomes; specs match contracts
- **Hiring** — strong PMs are scarce; project managers are not
- **Trust** — leadership doesn't trust teams to make the right calls
- **Org gravity** — large orgs default to specialization → feature teams emerge naturally

The shift to product teams requires executive-level cultural change, not just renaming roles.

## Diagnosing your team

Ask: **what would happen if my team ignored the next feature on the roadmap and worked on the underlying outcome instead?**

- "We'd get fired" → feature team
- "We'd be applauded if we moved the metric" → product team
- "We'd need 6 layers of approval" → feature team in product clothing

## Hybrid / partial states

Real orgs are messy. Common partial states:
- **Discovery theater** — interviews happen, but features are still pre-decided
- **Quarterly bets** — product-team behavior within a quarter, feature-team commits between quarters
- **Senior PM autonomy** — strong PMs operate as product teams; new ones are stuck on features
- **Platform = feature team, product = product team** — different parts of the org operate differently

## The Cagan move

To convert a feature team to a product team:
1. Replace specs with outcomes
2. Free 30–40% of capacity for discovery
3. Open customer access
4. Hire/develop strong PMs and tech leads
5. Coach managers from task-assigners to coaches

Months, not weeks. Most organizations underestimate the depth of change.
