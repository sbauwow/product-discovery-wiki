# Strong Product Manager

> Source: Marty Cagan (*Inspired*, *Empowered*), Lenny Rachitsky, Shreyas Doshi. The skill profile of a PM who can run discovery on an [empowered team](empowered-team.md). Distinct from project managers, business analysts, and "feature owners".

## The Cagan profile

A strong PM brings deep knowledge in **four** domains:

### 1. Knowledge of the user
Not generic empathy. Specific, evidence-based knowledge:
- Who the user is (segments, jobs)
- What they currently do
- Why they do it
- What frustrates them
- What they've tried

Built through [continuous discovery](continuous-discovery.md). A PM who can't tell stories about specific users by name has weak user knowledge.

### 2. Knowledge of the data
Quantitative literacy:
- How to read a [cohort](cohort-analysis.md) table
- How to design an [A/B test](ab-testing.md)
- How to spot vanity vs actionable metrics
- How to write a SQL query (or work fluently with someone who does)

PMs who can't open the data tools and pull a number themselves are slow and easily misled.

### 3. Knowledge of the business
- Revenue model
- Sales cycle
- Customer acquisition cost
- Lifetime value
- Brand constraints
- Legal / compliance constraints
- Competitive landscape

This is what separates PM from designer/engineer. A great designer cares about UX; a great PM also has to care that legal doesn't get sued and finance hits its margin.

### 4. Knowledge of the market and industry
- Competitor moves
- Adjacent technology trends
- Regulatory environment
- Sales channel dynamics

Cagan's claim: most "PMs" are weak in 2–3 of these four. Strong PMs are competent in all four.

## Beyond knowledge: skills

Knowledge is necessary, not sufficient. Skills:

| Skill | Manifests as |
|---|---|
| **Synthesis** | Reducing 50 interviews + dashboards into one clear narrative |
| **Judgment** | Picking which 3 things to do from 30 candidates |
| **Communication** | Making complex tradeoffs legible to engineers, designers, execs |
| **Influence without authority** | Aligning a team you don't manage |
| **Conviction with humility** | Strong opinions, weakly held — willingness to update |
| **Storytelling** | Framing the work so people care |
| **Saying no** | The most underrated PM skill |

## Shreyas Doshi's "LNO" framework

Allocate PM time:
- **L** — Leverage tasks (high impact, often hard) — discovery, strategy, hiring
- **N** — Neutral tasks — meetings, rituals, status updates
- **O** — Overhead tasks — JIRA, formatting, reporting

Strong PMs spend ≥50% on L. Weak PMs drown in N and O. The org pulls everyone toward N+O; strong PMs fight to protect L time.

## What strong PM is NOT

| ❌ Not | ✅ But |
|---|---|
| Project manager (tracks tasks) | Owner of outcome |
| Business analyst (writes specs) | Co-discoverer with team |
| Feature owner (named on roadmap) | Problem owner |
| Yes-person to execs | Pushes back with evidence |
| Technical (code is the goal) | Technically literate (engages eng meaningfully) |
| Designer (mocks UIs) | Co-designs with designer |

## Hiring signals (for hiring strong PMs)

In interviews, probe:

1. **Tell me about a feature you killed** — strong PMs have killed features. Weak PMs only ship.
2. **What's the metric you care about most? Why?** — looking for actionable, not vanity
3. **Tell me about a time you disagreed with your CEO** — looking for thoughtful pushback
4. **Walk me through the last user interview you did** — was it past-behavior probing or [solutioning](solutioning-trap.md)?
5. **What's your role's impact at your last job?** — outcome stories vs output lists
6. **How do you decide what NOT to build?** — explicit kill criteria vs "we just didn't"

## Common weak-PM patterns

- **Spec writer** — writes detailed PRDs, no discovery work upstream
- **Stakeholder taxi** — collects requests and routes them to engineering
- **Roadmap defender** — fights for shipping committed features regardless of evidence
- **Metric-less** — can't articulate what "success" looks like for current work
- **No conviction** — defers to whoever spoke last
- **Conviction without humility** — locks in early, ignores contradicting data

## Career arc

| Level | Focus | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Associate PM** | Learn the domain, ship features under direction | Features that work |
| **Product Manager** | Own one outcome | Outcome moved |
| **Senior PM** | Own multiple outcomes, mentor APMs | Sustained impact, junior development |
| **Group / Principal PM** | Own a product area or strategic problem | Strategic bets, ambiguity navigation |
| **Director / Head of Product** | Set strategy, hire/coach PMs | Org capability, multi-quarter outcomes |
| **VP / CPO** | Product org leadership | Whole-product success, executive interface |

Each level requires a step change in **judgment**, not just scope.
